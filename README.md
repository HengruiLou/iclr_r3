## Clinical Prevalence of Structural Pathological Events

Histological isomerism is not an independent disease entity, but describes a diagnostic ambiguity underlying structural pathological events. Such events are clinically prevalent across multiple malignancies:

- **Perineural Invasion (PNI) in colorectal cancer:** A systematic review of 58 studies involving 22,900 patients reported an incidence of **18.2%** [1].
- **PNI in gastric cancer:** A meta-analysis of 13 studies involving 7,004 patients reported an incidence of **35.9%** [2].
- **Lymphovascular Invasion (LVI) in colorectal cancer:** A large NCDB cohort reported LVI in **26.3% of 139,026 patients** with documented LVI status [3].
- **Extramural Vascular Invasion (EMVI) in rectal cancer:** A meta-analysis of 14 studies involving 7,262 patients reported a pooled prevalence of **26%** [4].

These findings highlight the clinical relevance of structural pathological events across different malignancies. Their diagnosis depends not merely on the presence of tumor cells and anatomical structures, but on their spatial relationships.
